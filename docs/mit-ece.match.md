## 1) What MIT emphasizes for incoming students (plain English)
MIT’s first-year admissions guidance repeatedly frames selection as a “match” rather than a formula. They want students who show that they’ll thrive in an environment that is intensely hands-on (“Mind and Hand”), collaboration-centered (team problem-solving and cross-lab work), and opportunity-driven (initiative to pursue labs/makerspaces/learning paths).  

For academic readiness, MIT focuses on whether you’re prepared for the General Institute Requirements: strong quantitative foundations—especially calculus—and enough science preparation to start the Science Core work successfully.  

For an EE/robotics/autonomous systems fit specifically, the EECS curriculum context matters: EECS introduces fundamentals through concrete systems (explicitly mentioning robots and other tangible engineering contexts) and structures later depth with advanced subjects tied to subdisciplines like control/signal processing and AI—so they’re likely looking for an engineering story that connects your interests to building, testing, and iterating on real systems.
