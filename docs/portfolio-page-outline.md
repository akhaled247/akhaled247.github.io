
# Portfolio project page — writing outline

Use this in order when drafting or revising any project page (`_pages/.../*.md`).

---

## 0. Before you write

- **Audience**: Generalist reader first (many admissions readers are not subject experts).
- **Goal**: Verify and deepen what’s already on the application—**not** replace Activities or essays.
- **One line**: Decide the single idea this page should leave behind (e.g. “I owned vision + localization and validated it under competition constraints”).

---

## 1. Front matter

### `title`

- Project name + scope (season, course, or year) if relevant.
- Specific; avoid vague titles (“Robotics project”).

### `description` (meta / preview)

- **What** the project is (one short phrase).
- **Your role** (build, lead subsystem, solo, etc.).
- **One proof** (method, subsystem, outcome, or artifact)—not hype.

---

## 2. Opening (first screen)

- **Context**: What problem or goal, in plain language.
- **Your contribution**: What *you* did vs team (especially for group work).
- **Why it matters**: User, competition, learning, or community—one concrete reason.
- **Jargon**: Define acronyms once; keep technical depth for the next sections.

---

## 3. Core narrative (body)

Work through these in whatever order fits the project; include every item that applies.

### A. Problem and goals

- What you were trying to achieve and for whom (if anyone).

### B. Constraints

- Time, rules (e.g. competition), budget, hardware, course requirements, safety.

### C. What you built or owned

- Subsystems, features, experiments, or deliverables **you** were responsible for.

### D. How it works (accessible → detailed)

- High-level explanation first, then deeper technical detail for readers who want it.

### E. Validation / evidence

- Tests, metrics, logs, competition results, grades/rubric if course, mentor or judge feedback.
- **Links**: repo, CAD, notebook, video, paper—anything a skeptical reader could skim.

### F. Iteration / failure

- One concrete “what broke / what didn’t work → what you changed” story.

### G. Collaboration and leadership (if applicable)

- Who else was involved; how decisions were made; mentoring, documentation, onboarding.

### H. Ethics / safety / responsibility (if applicable)

- Data, users, health, fairness, shop safety—only where real.

### I. Outcomes / impact (honest scale)

- What changed: skills, team, users, rankings, deployment—stated specifically, not only superlatives.

### J. Reflection (short)

- What you learned that class didn’t give you, or what you’d do differently next time (1–3 sentences).

---

## 4. Media

- Images / diagrams / video: each tied to **what it proves** (caption-level sentence).
- Prefer clarity over volume.

---

## 5. Calls to action (optional)

- Buttons or links: GitHub, demo, PDF, external article—only if stable and relevant.

---

## 6. `/projects/` card (`excerpt` in `projects.md`)

- **Hook** for someone browsing the index.
- Complement `description`: e.g. one emphasizes topic, the other emphasizes role + proof.
- Avoid contradicting the page title/body.

---

## 7. Final pass (checklist)

- [ ] First screen understandable in ~30 seconds for a non-expert.
- [ ] Your role is explicit on team projects.
- [ ] At least one **verifiable** detail (test, metric, artifact, link).
- [ ] At least one **iteration or failure** story (if anything went wrong—it usually did).
- [ ] `title`, `description`, body, and `excerpt` agree and don’t duplicate fluff.

---

## 8. Adjust by page type (pick emphasis)

| Type | Extra emphasis |
|------|----------------|
| **Competition / robotics** | Season arc, subsystem ownership, rules, testing, mentoring, docs. |
| **Course / lab / PLTW** | Objectives, apparatus, measurement/uncertainty, safety, reproducibility. |
| **Personal / hobby** | Motivation, constraints, stack choices, maintenance, users or OSS if any. |
